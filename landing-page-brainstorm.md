# Product Landing Page Brainstorm

This draft assumes a modern software product with a primary conversion goal of getting visitors to start a trial, request a demo, or join a waitlist.

## Positioning Options

### Option 1: Clear and Trustworthy

Best for products that need to feel credible, useful, and easy to adopt.

- Core idea: reduce friction and explain the benefit fast
- Tone: calm, competent, specific
- Best CTA: Start free

Hero direction:

> The simplest way to turn complex work into a repeatable system.

Supporting subhead:

> `Product` helps teams move faster by removing the manual steps, scattered tools, and avoidable errors that slow down everyday work.

### Option 2: Bold and Outcome-Driven

Best for products replacing slow, frustrating, manual workflows.

- Core idea: contrast the old way with the new way
- Tone: decisive, modern, energetic
- Best CTA: See it in action

Hero direction:

> Stop doing this the hard way.

Supporting subhead:

> `Product` automates the busywork, keeps everyone aligned, and helps your team get from idea to outcome without the usual drag.

### Option 3: Premium and Serious

Best for products in higher-trust categories like finance, infrastructure, legal, or enterprise software.

- Core idea: confidence, control, reliability
- Tone: polished, understated, assured
- Best CTA: Book a demo

Hero direction:

> Built for teams that need clarity, speed, and control.

Supporting subhead:

> `Product` gives your team a dependable way to manage critical work without compromise, chaos, or unnecessary complexity.

## Recommended Page Structure

### 1. Hero

Use one headline, one supporting line, one primary CTA, and one simple proof point.

Example:

- Headline: The fastest way to make your workflow actually flow.
- Subhead: Replace manual handoffs, scattered updates, and avoidable delays with one system your team will actually use.
- Primary CTA: Start free
- Secondary CTA: Watch demo
- Proof point: Trusted by hundreds of modern teams

### 2. Social Proof Bar

Show credibility immediately below the hero.

Examples:

- Used by fast-moving product, ops, and revenue teams
- Cuts admin time by up to 60%
- Works with the tools teams already rely on

### 3. Problem Section

Describe the current pain in concrete language.

Example copy:

> Most teams do important work across too many places. Updates get buried, ownership gets fuzzy, and progress depends on people remembering to chase the next step.

Follow with contrast:

> `Product` replaces that friction with one clear workflow, so work moves forward without constant follow-up.

### 4. How It Works

Keep this to three steps.

Example:

1. Connect your workflow
2. Automate the repetitive parts
3. Track progress in one clear place

### 5. Benefits Section

Lead with outcomes, not feature names.

- Move faster without adding headcount
- Reduce manual errors and missed steps
- Give every stakeholder one shared source of truth
- Launch with less coordination overhead

### 6. Product or UI Showcase

Use a screenshot, mockup, or simple product animation.

Suggested captions:

- Everything important, visible at a glance
- Built for real workflows, not idealized demos
- Fast enough for daily use, flexible enough for growing teams

### 7. Testimonials or Results

Use proof with specifics whenever possible.

Examples:

> "We cut our weekly admin overhead in half within the first month."

> "The biggest improvement was alignment. Everyone knew what was happening without asking."

### 8. Objection Handling

Answer the questions that block conversion.

- Is setup complicated?
- Will this work with our current tools?
- Is this for small teams, larger teams, or both?
- Can we try it before committing?

Example FAQ answer:

> Most teams can get started in less than an hour. The goal is to simplify your existing workflow, not force a complete rebuild.

### 9. Final CTA

Repeat the promise with slightly more urgency.

Example:

- Headline: Replace friction with momentum.
- Subhead: Start with one workflow, prove the value fast, and expand from there.
- CTA: Start free

## Headline Ideas

- Turn messy work into clear progress.
- The workflow upgrade your team will actually notice.
- Less coordination. More forward motion.
- One system for the work that matters most.
- Replace scattered process with visible progress.
- Move faster without adding more tools to manage.

## Visual Direction

### Direction A: Clean SaaS

- Bright background
- Strong typography
- Large centered product UI
- Minimal palette with one strong accent color

### Direction B: Editorial Bold

- Larger headline scale
- Asymmetric layout
- Strong contrast between text blocks and product visuals
- More attitude in the copy

### Direction C: Premium Minimal

- Tighter palette
- More whitespace
- Softer motion
- Trust-first design details and restrained copy

## Copy Formula

Use this to adapt the draft later:

> For `audience`, `Product` helps you `desired outcome` without `main pain point`.

Examples:

- For operations teams, `Product` helps you standardize execution without creating more process overhead.
- For startup teams, `Product` helps you move from planning to action without losing visibility.
